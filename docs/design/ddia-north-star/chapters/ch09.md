# Chapter 9. The Trouble with Distributed Systems / Consistency and Consensus (lite atlas)

## One-sentence thesis

Partial failure is normal; consistency and consensus are tools with costs — most of this product should avoid needing them.

## Who this chapter is for

Anyone proposing distributed locking or multi-primary truth for this product.

## What it covers

Failure modes and consistency vocabulary (lite).

## When to apply

Before introducing cross-node coordination.

## Where it shows up in systems

Rare in this monorepo meta path; more relevant in target distributed Spring systems.

## Why it matters

Consensus theater is a common band-aid for unfixed single-writer design.

## How to use it here

Default to domain 01 solutions; deepen this domain before relying on it (`partial`).

## Section map

### Faults and Partial Failures

- Partial failure is normal in distributed systems; design for it.
- Fail if a control assumes all-or-nothing success across fan-out without timeouts.

### Unreliable Networks

- Networks delay, reorder, and drop; timeouts are guesses.
- Fail if infinite wait / no deadline is treated as 'reliable enough'.

### Consistency Guarantees (survey)

- Consistency words are overloaded; cite the concrete guarantee.
- Fail if 'consistent' is used without naming the model (`consistency-and-consensus-lite`).

### Consensus (survey)

- Consensus is hard; most product paths should avoid needing it.
- Fail if consensus is introduced where recompute-from-SoR would suffice.

## Digested claims

- Timeouts are guesses; networks lie.
- Strong consistency needs coordination cost.
- Prefer designs that need less consensus (single SoR writer).
