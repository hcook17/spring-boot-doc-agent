## Summary

One commit. A review reported `scripts/` as hard to follow; the useful part of that review was that it is **not sloppiness — it is density, with the justification placed before the mechanism.**

Measured across 35 module docstrings: **1,481 lines, mean 42.** `spring_drift_check.py` ran to 202 lines with its usage block at **line 194**; `spring_signal_scan.py` to 152 with **none at all**. Nine modules had no usage block; fourteen buried it past line 20.

**The density is an asset and is preserved.** `.ruff.toml` sets this repo's line limit from its own prose distribution deliberately. Nothing here deletes reasoning — it is reordered, and that was verified rather than asserted: every substantive sentence of the three restructured docstrings still appears verbatim, except the one-line summaries the contract asks to be rewritten. `spring_signal_scan.py` lost **zero** sentences.

Three parts: the contract in `CONTRIBUTING.md` (*what* → *how to run* → *why*), enforcement in `check_code_quality.py`, and the three worst offenders restructured (violations 18 → 15).

## Two judgement calls a reviewer should check rather than assume

**Library modules are exempt by construction.** `doc_tag_utils.py` and the three underscore helpers are imported, never run. A check demanding a command from them would be pointed at the wrong thing — this repo's own "a validator that validates fixtures" anti-pattern, one step removed.

**Moving the essays to `docs/` was rejected.** This repo's dominant failure mode is prose drifting from code — prompt `07`'s stale `status:`, `CONSTRAINTS.md` citing a deleted script, `12` naming files that did not exist. A standalone rationale doc is the highest-drift-risk location available, and it is exactly what `check_repo_claims.py` exists to catch. Keep it in the file; invert the order.

## A defect this work introduced, caught before shipping

`measure_tree()` globbed `scripts/*.py`, so regenerating the baseline while a concurrent session's untracked files were present captured **93 of their functions** and raised the annotation floor to 35.4% against a committed tree measuring 23.4% — CI would have failed on its first run, reporting a regression caused entirely by files that were never committed. Fixed at the cause: the baseline describes the *committed* tree, so `measure_tree()` reads `git ls-files` and falls back to the glob outside a checkout.

## On the threshold, so it is not over-trusted

`USAGE_WITHIN_LINES = 20` sits in an 11-line gap in this repo's bimodal distribution (twelve modules orient by line 18; thirteen bury it at 29+; nothing between). In the threshold-derivation literature's terms that is **unsupervised natural-breaks clustering on a single system, n=25** — the weakest available basis. The canonical unsupervised method (Alves, Ypma & Visser, ICSM 2010) aggregates across ~100 systems *precisely because* single-system thresholds are unstable; the only outcome signal here is n=1. Recorded as a fact about the current population with a re-derivation command in `CONTRIBUTING.md`, not a constant to defend.

## Not touched

`scripts/check_repo_claims.py` is a concurrent session's **untracked** work and was not edited. Findings for its author are in `claude/check-repo-claims-review-2026-07-25.md`.
